# Stage 0, measurement 3: classical OCR as reference

**28 July 2026** · The bar every vision model has to justify itself against.
Without it, “better than OCR” is a claim without a number.

## Set-up

| | |
|---|---|
| Corpus | 10 synthetic documents × 4 scan qualities = 40 pages |
| Ground truth | text layer of the PDF the scan was rasterised from — exact to the character |
| Machine | x86_64, 16 cores available, **4 threads used** |
| Engines | Tesseract 5.3.4 (`deu`, tessdata_best) · RapidOCR 1.4.4 (ONNX) |

Four quality levels, from “freshly printed” to “third copy”: `clean`
(300 dpi, unchanged), `good` (300 dpi, slight noise and skew), `medium`
(150 dpi, noise, skew, uneven lighting), `poor` (150 dpi, heavy noise, 2°
skew, loss of contrast). The raw JSON records them under their original
German names `sauber`, `gut`, `mittel` and `schlecht`.

## Metrics

**Hit rate** is the number that matters here: the share of reference words
found again in the OCR result. It is searched as a substring of the text
without spaces — independent of order, because grounding runs through the
word index and region extraction through coordinates.

**CER_seq / WER_seq** are the classic rates of the OCR literature. On
multi-column documents they mostly measure reading order and are given here
only for context — see “What the naive metric hides”.

## Result

| Engine | Quality | dpi | Hits | worst document | CER_seq | WER_seq | s/page | reported confidence |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **tesseract** | clean | 300 | **98.9%** | 97.8% | 0.2% | 2.4% | 0.71 | 94% |
| **tesseract** | good | 300 | **98.9%** | 97.6% | 0.2% | 2.4% | 0.73 | 94% |
| **tesseract** | medium | 150 | **98.9%** | 97.6% | 0.1% | 1.1% | 0.48 | 93% |
| **tesseract** | poor | 150 | **98.8%** | 96.6% | 0.3% | 2.1% | 0.51 | 92% |
| rapidocr | clean | 300 | 90.5% | 82.7% | 3.4% | 30.5% | 1.04 | 99% |
| rapidocr | good | 300 | 89.8% | 86.8% | 5.8% | 53.4% | 1.04 | 99% |
| rapidocr | medium | 150 | 88.6% | 83.1% | 6.7% | 60.1% | 0.81 | 98% |
| rapidocr | poor | 150 | 87.3% | 81.9% | 9.1% | 58.1% | 0.80 | 97% |

## Four findings

**1. Tesseract clearly beats RapidOCR — contrary to the plan.**
98.9% against 90.5% on clean scans, 98.8% against 87.3% on poor ones. The
technical analysis had RapidOCR as the primary engine and Tesseract as “weak
on poor scans”. As measured, the opposite holds.

**2. Tesseract is remarkably robust.** From `clean` to `poor` the hit rate
drops by 0.1 percentage points — 98.9% to 98.8%. Noise, skew and loss of
contrast do not bother it. RapidOCR loses 3.2 points over the same step.

**3. Tesseract is faster.** 0.48–0.73 s/page against 0.80–1.04. Both are
within the range estimated by the technical analysis (0.5–3 s/page), at its
lower end. Notably, 150 dpi is faster *and* equally accurate as 300 dpi for
Tesseract — higher resolution gains nothing here.

**4. RapidOCR's confidence is useless as a control value.** It reports
97–99% at an actual 87–90% hits. Tesseract reports 92–94% at 98.9% — it
understates. This hits the planned architecture directly: “The measured
confidence decides which stage applies.” With RapidOCR as the confidence
source, the reading stage would never escalate, although every tenth word is
missing.

## What the naive metric hides

The first measurement attempt compared reference and OCR text character by
character and gave Tesseract 9.4% character errors on a *clean* 300 dpi
image. The re-check showed: **83 of 83 words recognised correctly**, a single
character wrong (`·` as `-`).

The 9.4% were reading order. Tesseract puts the totals block at the end and
reads it column by column; the PDF text layer delivers it in the middle and
row by row. For Substrakt this does not matter — grounding and region
extraction are independent of order.

Hence the hit rate as the main number. Anyone comparing this measurement with
others must watch the metric: the usual CER figures from benchmarks measure
something else on multi-column documents than here.

A second finding from the same re-check: **RapidOCR loses word boundaries.**
It returns `Rheinland ElektrotechnikGmbH` and `IndustriestraBe 47·24103Kiel`
— 76 instead of 83 words. For a word bounding-box index that is a problem
beyond pure character accuracy.

## Recommendation

**Tesseract 5 as primary engine**, RapidOCR as an optional second opinion —
the reverse of the previous plan.

This matches the licence audit, which already credits Tesseract with the
cleaner licence chain (code Apache-2.0, `deu.traineddata` from tessdata_best
verified Apache-2.0; the PP-OCR weights carry no model-specific licence file
with the artefact). The conflict suspected there between legal certainty and
“the last points of accuracy” does not exist: both criteria point to
Tesseract.

For confidence-driven escalation, **Tesseract's word confidence** is to be
used. A threshold around 85% seems sensible — at 92–94% in the normal case
there is room below without good pages escalating needlessly. The value is to
be checked as soon as real scans are available.

## Limitations

**The corpus is synthetic.** Generated documents are typographically cleaner
than scanned reality: no folds, stains, stamps, staples, carbon copies or
handwritten notes. The absolute numbers are an **upper bound**. For comparing
the engines with each other and for the timing this does not matter — both
get the same images.

**4 threads on a 16-core machine.** This approximates a laptop but does not
replace a measurement on the target hardware. With fewer cores and slower
memory, higher times are to be expected.

**RapidOCR 1.4.4** brings its own ONNX models. Whether they are the latest
PP-OCRv5 weights was not checked — with more recent models the result could
differ.

## Side finding for the bundle

Setting `TESSDATA_PREFIX` to a model directory of our own is not enough:
Tesseract also looks for its `configs/` there. Without them every output
format except plain text fails — including the TSV output with the
confidence values, which later drives the escalation. The `configs/`
directory belongs in the bundle.

## Reproducing

```bash
cd sidecar
uv run python ../corpus/measurement_corpus.py   # 10 documents × 4 qualities, fixed seed
uv run python ../bench/ocr.py --threads 4 --write
```
